# Gluten-Free Airfryer Churros

## Ingredients

### Choux pastry dough
- 125 ml low-fat milk, 1.5% fat
- 125 ml water
- 65 g butter, cut into chunks for faster melting
- 10 g sugar, about 1 level tbsp
- 10 g vanilla sugar, 1 packet
- 2 g salt, about 1/3 tsp
- 250 g Schär Mix B (Mix Brot) bread flour blend, gluten-free, weighed out in advance
- 3-4 eggs, size M

### Airfryer and finishing
- 2-3 tbsp neutral cooking oil, e.g. canola or sunflower oil, for brushing/spraying
- 25 g butter, melted, for brushing after baking
- 60 g sugar, for coating
- 2 tsp ground cinnamon, for coating

## Instructions

1. **Boil the liquids and butter** – Add the milk (125 ml), water (125 ml), butter (65 g), sugar (10 g), vanilla sugar (10 g) and salt (2 g) to a medium-sized pot. Heat over medium-high heat (e.g. level 7 of 9), stirring occasionally, until the butter is completely melted and the liquid reaches a rolling boil. Don't use maximum heat, or the milk will burn on the bottom.
2. **Add the flour and cook the dough** – Briefly take the pot off the heat and turn the stove down to low (level 2–3). Pour in all of the Schär Mix B (250 g) at once and immediately stir vigorously with a sturdy wooden spoon or silicone spatula until the mass is smooth with no dry flour pockets. Return the pot to the low heat and cook the dough for 1–2 minutes, constantly pressing and stirring. It should form a smooth ball that pulls away from the sides and leaves a thin, whitish film on the bottom of the pot.
3. **Cool to lukewarm** – Transfer the hot dough ball to a mixing bowl right away to stop the cooking and keep it from drying out. Let it cool for 5–10 minutes until just lukewarm to the touch; stirring it a few times with a hand mixer on the lowest speed speeds this up. Don't let it cool completely (30–60 minutes): the plant fibers in the gluten-free flour swell too much and the dough becomes too stiff to take the eggs.
4. **Incorporate the eggs** – Crack 4 eggs into a small bowl or measuring cup and whisk lightly with a fork. With the dough hooks of a hand mixer (or the paddle of a stand mixer) on medium speed, add the eggs to the lukewarm dough about one egg's worth at a time, mixing until completely smooth before adding more. The dough may look slimy or separated at first – keep mixing until it comes back together.
5. **Test the consistency** – After about three-quarters of the egg (roughly 3 eggs), stop and check the dough. It should be smooth, glossy and thick but creamy; when you pull the mixer out, a soft peak should form and slowly bend over without losing its shape. If it is still too stiff or crumbly, mix in the rest of the egg bit by bit until it reaches that consistency.
6. **Preheat and pipe** – Preheat the Airfryer to 190 °C (375 °F). Put perforated Airfryer parchment paper in the basket and rub it very lightly with oil. Work quickly so the dough doesn't get too tough to pipe. Fill half of the dough into a piping bag with a large star nozzle (10–12 mm), or a sturdy freezer bag with a 1–1.5 cm corner snipped off. Pipe 8–10 cm strips onto the paper, leaving space between them, and cut the ends cleanly with a knife or oiled fingers. If shaping by hand or with spoons, press light ridges lengthwise into the dough with a fork so it expands evenly without bursting.
7. **Oil and airfry** – Brush or spray the churros lightly on all sides with oil. Bake at 190 °C (375 °F) for 10–12 minutes, flipping them carefully after 5–6 minutes (brush dry spots with a little more oil). Bake until golden brown and crispy all around.
8. **Butter and cinnamon sugar** – While the first batch bakes, mix the coating sugar (60 g) and cinnamon (2 tsp) on a flat plate. As soon as the churros are done, brush them very thinly with melted butter while still piping hot, then immediately roll them in the cinnamon sugar so it sticks.
9. **Repeat** – Keep the remaining dough covered with plastic wrap or a plate so it doesn't dry out. Repeat piping, baking and coating until all the dough is used – usually 3–4 batches, depending on the size of the basket.

## Notes

- Frying vs. airfrying: the traditional Schär recipe uses no fat in the dough because it's meant for deep-frying. Adding the 65 g of butter to the boiling liquid is crucial for the Airfryer method; it makes the churros tender inside and golden brown outside.
- Yield / batches: about 20–25 churros. They need space for air circulation in the basket, so bake them in several batches. "4 servings" is the total yield.
- Troubleshooting: if the dough is too soft and loses its ridges right after piping, chill the piped churros in the fridge for 15–20 minutes before airfrying so they hold their shape.
- Gluten check: Schär Mix B is certified gluten-free. Vanilla sugar is normally gluten-free, but check the label – some brands use starch carriers.
